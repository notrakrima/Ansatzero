What We Do

Four Pillars. One Pipeline.

The Institute operates as a high-performance research pipeline, designed to move fundamental discoveries to real-world breakthroughs with maximum velocity.

1. Generative AI

The Discovery Engine

We use closed-source and tailored open-source Generative AI models capable of generating novel scientific hypotheses and synthesizing massive datasets. This capability expands the frontier of what is possible, driving breakthrough discoveries across all target sectors of industry, government, and research.

2. Predictive AI

Advanced Pattern Recognition

We deploy predictive AI models that efficiently calculate physical properties, analyze vast datasets, and map complex relationships. Our predictive systems augment standard computational models by utilizing historical patterns to generate rapid, highly informed projections for complex, large-scale challenges.

3. Agentic Workflows

The Autonomous Loop

We combine generative and predictive AI models to build autonomous agentic AI frameworks that generate hypotheses, test them against historical data, learn from the results, and iterate. By orchestrating this continuous feedback loop, our agentic systems streamline complex R&D pipelines and rapidly filter candidates before physical resources are ever expended.

4. Physical Validation

The Reality Check

AI predictions are meaningless without physical proof. We hand off our highest-confidence AI outputs directly into "self-driving" automated laboratories and real-world deployment pipelines. By directly linking our agentic software frameworks to robotic synthesis and physical validation platforms, we close the loop, translating virtual projections into tangible, high-TRL outcomes.

Flagship Project

AI-Driven Solar Panel Recycling

The Sovereign Bottleneck

By 2030, Australia will generate 91,000 tonnes of end-of-life solar panel waste annually, representing over $1 billion in locked material value. While standard mechanical recycling can recover 95% of a panel's mass, the high-purity silicon wafers and the silver remain securely bonded to weather-resistant substrates. Removing this silicon without destroying its purity is a bottleneck for the renewable circular economy.

Accelerating Traditional R&D

Finding solvent combinations capable of breaking the bonds between the silicon wafers and the substrate using traditional laboratory approaches may take years of trial-and-error. ISA accelerates this discovery by deploying our fully autonomous AI architecture. Moving beyond human intuition, our predictive AI models screen thousands of quantum chemical formulations to identify optimal molecular targets capable of cleanly isolating the silicon.

Closing the Autonomous Loop

Our agentic workflows orchestrate this pipeline, handing off high-confidence solvent candidates directly to a $2.7M ARC-funded automated robotic laboratory. The robotic platform physically executes the synthesis and feeds experimental validation data directly back into the AI engine. This closed-loop system actively steers the discovery of scalable, efficient, and non-toxic recycling pathways at unprecedented speed.

Translation to Industry

In collaboration with major renewable developers, this capability translates advanced computational science into regional value. By engineering scalable recycling technologies designed for localized deployment within Renewable Energy Zones (REZs), the Institute is converting a national waste crisis into a sovereign, high-value asset.

Our Team

Professor Amir Karton

Director, Institute for Strategic AI

Professor of Physical and Materials Chemistry

Professor Amir Karton is the Founding Director of the Institute for Strategic AI (ISA) and Associate Dean of Research at the University of New England (UNE), alongside appointments as a Visiting Researcher at Microsoft Research (AI4Science) and an Adjunct Professor at The University of Western Australia. Operating at the intersection of AI and quantum mechanics, he develops agentic workflows and scientific foundation models to accelerate autonomous materials discovery.

By integrating generative, predictive, and agentic AI, his team builds autonomous workflows that overcome traditional research bottlenecks and dramatically accelerate both academic discovery and industrial innovation.

The significance of his foundational contributions to the physical sciences has been recognized by prestigious Australian Research Council Fellowships (DECRA and Future Fellowship), the Le Fèvre Medal from the Australian Academy of Science, the American Chemical Society (ACS) PHYS Division Lectureship Award, the Royal Australian Chemical Institute (RACI) Physical Chemistry Lectureship and Citation Award, and the UNE Research Collaboration Leadership Award. Professor Karton is an Editor for Chemical Physics Letters and is an Associate Editor for the Australian Journal of Chemistry. He is the Vice President of the Association of Molecular Modellers of Australasia (AMMA) and a Board Member of the Australian Association of Theoretical and Computational Chemists (AATACC).

Associate Professor Aaron Driver

Chief AI Officer, University of New England

Director, LabNext70

Associate Professor Aaron Driver is the Founding Deputy Director of the Institute for Strategic AI (ISA). Aaron leads LabNext70, UNE's applied AI innovation lab, where he drives AI integration across teaching, research, and operations. His background spans journalism, technology, marketing, and behavioural science, giving him a rare ability to bridge technical capability with strategic narrative and real-world deployment.

At UNE, Aaron has been instrumental in building the university's sovereign AI platform and pioneering AI-native approaches to education and academic integrity. He specialises in social marketing, narrative persuasion, and behaviour change, with particular expertise in climate change and biosecurity communications.

He brings deep expertise in industry partnership development and the translation of research into practical, high-impact applications, ensuring the Institute's work aligns with UNE's broader strategic vision.
