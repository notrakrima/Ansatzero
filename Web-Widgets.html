The Problem

Too Many Emails. Not Enough Answers.

Every collaborative project has a bottleneck: the expression of interest phase. Potential partners need quick estimates — how long will this take? How much data? What resources? — but the answers require specialist knowledge locked inside spreadsheets and expert heads.

The result? An inbox full of the same questions. Weeks of back-and-forth. Decision paralysis. And the people who should be doing science are instead answering FAQs.

AI Web Widgets solve this by putting the estimator directly in the hands of the community — a self-service tool that gives instant, informed answers without replacing the formal processes that follow.

Case Study

AVIRIS-3 Oz Flight Planner

A pre-flight estimation tool built for the 2026 NASA AVIRIS imaging spectroscopy campaign to Australia — deployed during the expression of interest phase to help potential collaborators scope their flight requests.

● The Context

In the lead-up to the 2026 NASA JPL AVIRIS deployment across Australia, dozens of research groups, government agencies, and industry partners needed to submit expressions of interest for flight time over their study sites. Every group had the same questions:

"How many flight hours will my site need?"
Depends on area, GSD, overlap, terrain, and solar constraints.
"How many cloud-free days do we need?"
Depends on total flight time vs. available solar window per day.
"How much data will be generated?"
Depends on science hours at ~20 GB/hr average rate.

Answering these individually for every collaborator was consuming weeks of specialist time. The flight planner was built to put the estimator directly in the hands of the community.

● The Solution

Prof. Bradley Evans built the AVIRIS-3 Oz Flight Planner — a single HTML file that collaborators could open in any browser, upload their study area boundary (KML/KMZ), configure sensor parameters, and instantly receive:

  • → 3D terrain-following flight lines — clipped precisely to the uploaded polygon boundary, with real elevation data from the Open-Meteo API
  • → Mission time estimates — science hours, turn/repositioning time, transit to nearest Jet A1 airport (from a built-in Australian airport database), and total flight hours
  • → Solar timing constraints — configurable solar window with Zulu time calculations based on the site's longitude
  • → Cloud-free day estimates and data volume projections
  • → Downloadable 3D KML flight plan — viewable in Google Earth with terrain-following altitude profiles

An Estimator, Not a Replacement

The flight planner is a pre-flight estimation tool designed for the expression of interest phase. It does not replace existing flight planning procedures, which are mandatory for safety, regulatory compliance, and operational approval. It sits in the delivery chain as a scoping aid — giving collaborators an informed starting point before formal mission planning begins.

The Delivery Chain

Where Widgets Fit

A web widget doesn't replace your workflow — it enhances a specific friction point. Here's where the flight planner sits in the AVIRIS campaign delivery chain:

1

Call for Expressions of Interest

Campaign announced. Research groups identify study sites and begin scoping their requirements.

Self-Service Estimation (Widget)

Collaborators upload their KML boundary, configure GSD and overlap, and instantly receive flight time, data volume, and cloud-free day estimates. No emails required.

3

Informed EOI Submission

Groups submit expressions of interest with realistic scope — they already know the approximate flight hours, data volumes, and scheduling constraints.

4

Formal Flight Planning & Safety

Approved sites proceed to mandatory formal flight planning — CASA compliance, pilot briefings, weather windows, and operational safety procedures. The widget's estimates are a starting point, not a substitute.
